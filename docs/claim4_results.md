# Claim 4 results: does our GPU rental index lead compute-linked equities?

First evaluation, run 2026-10-06 at 02:48 UTC with data through the
2026-10-05 close. The plan (`docs/claim4_plan.md`) was committed before any
test-window return was seen. Full tables are in
`reports/claim4/evaluation.md`, regenerated by every daily run.

## Bottom line

**The evidence neither supports nor rejects claim 4.** This was stated in
the plan before any result existed.

- **Too little data.** 32 sessions can only detect a correlation of about
  0.48 or more between an index change and the next session's excess
  return. After the multiple-testing correction the bar is about 0.61.
  Realistic effects for daily returns are 0.1 or less.
- **Nothing passed.** None of the 18 primary tests, 6 event tests or 18
  walk-forward forecasts passes.
- **Shadow mode.** The validation gate failed for all 18 signal and horizon
  pairs, so paper trading runs in shadow mode. The first shadow prediction
  is logged for the 2026-10-06 session. No order has been sent.
- **One secondary flag, not a finding.** One secondary test passes the 10%
  false-discovery screen: the neocloud basket against the B200 level change,
  next session, correlation −0.42. It rests on two sessions. Without them
  the correlation is −0.12 (post-hoc check below). It is worth watching. It
  is not evidence.

| Bar check | On SPY, 2026-10-02 (outside the study), the daily open matched the 09:30 regular-session open exactly. The daily close was within 0.13 bp of the last regular-session minute. So open-to-close returns are prices one could have traded at. Daily volume includes extended hours |
| Outcome scale (descriptive) | Daily excess return of the basket over XLK: standard deviation 1.86% (neocloud 2.93%, semiconductors 1.17%, power and capacity 2.12%). Mean −0.18% a session over the 32 sessions |

### How often the signals moved

Across the 32 sessions:

- **H100 level:** changed on 5 sessions.
- **B200 level:** changed on 11, 8 of them small +0.5% steps.
- **H100 dispersion:** changed on 26 sessions.
- **B200 dispersion:** changed on 6.
- **Availability:** changed on 3 sessions for H100 and 1 for B200.

Large level moves of 2% or more happened on two sessions per GPU:

| GPU | Session | Move |
|---|---|---|
| H100 | 09-04 | −3.3% |
| H100 | 10-01 | +2.4% |
| B200 | 09-04 | −5.5% |
| B200 | 10-05 | +10.4% |

The 10-05 B200 move came with panel listings rising from 8 to 11 when the
source widened its coverage. Part of it is probably composition, not price.

## What was run

All 114 declared tests and evaluations were run. The full list is in
`docs/variants_log.md`.

| Family | Tests | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| P (primary) | 18 | Lead-lag regressions: 6 signals × horizons of 1, 5 and 20 sessions. Newey-West t-test, Holm correction, circular-shift permutation |
| E | 6 | Event study on moves of 2% or more |
| W | 18 | Walk-forward forecasts against two naive baselines, zero and the running mean; Clark-West test |
| G3 | 18 | Net-of-cost strategy evaluations |
| S (secondary) | 54 | Lead-lag per bucket, Benjamini-Hochberg at 10% |

Settings:

- **Bootstrap.** Circular block bootstrap, 2,000 resamples, seed 20261005.
- **Costs.** All assumptions (plan section 7): 15 bps per side on stocks,
  3 bps on XLK, 1 bp of fees on sells, borrow at 5% a year on short stocks
  and 0.5% on short XLK. That puts a round trip of the long-short pair at
  about 0.38%.

## Results by family

### Family P, primary lead-lag: 0 of 18 pass

| Signal | Next-session correlation | 95% CI | Holm p | Non-zero sessions | Why it does not pass |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| level_h100 | −0.41 | −0.60 to 0.17 | 0.12 | 5 | Holm p above 0.05; fewer than 10 non-zero values |
| level_b200 | −0.35 | −0.61 to 0.03 | 0.95 | 11 | Holm p; permutation p 0.06 |
| disp_h100 | −0.04 | −0.28 to 0.21 | 1.00 | 26 | No effect |
| disp_b200 | 0.44 | −0.18 to 0.61 | 0.02 | 6 | Fewer than 10 non-zero values |
| avail_h100 | −0.26 | −0.43 to −0.08 | 0.38 | 3 | Fewer than 10 non-zero values |
| avail_b200 | −0.14 | −0.28 to −0.11 | 0.01 | 1 | Fewer than 10 non-zero values |

- **5-session horizon.** Nothing passes. Point correlations lie between
  −0.36 and 0.13.
- **20-session horizon.** No test can be computed: there are 13 overlapping
  windows, and a Newey-West test at that horizon needs at least 60 (D34).
- **Misleading small p-values.** Signals with very few non-zero values
  produce tiny p-values. For example, `avail_b200` has one non-zero session
  and a Holm p of 0.01. One point fits its own line exactly, which shrinks
  the standard error. The plan's rule of at least 10 non-zero values exists
  for this case. It is why `disp_b200` and `avail_b200` do not pass.

### Family E, event study: 0 of 6 pass, and none can

Each GPU had two events at the 1-session horizon. Mean signed excess return
on the next session:

- H100: −2.7%
- B200: −3.1%

In both cases the excess return went against the index move: after a rise
the basket did worse than XLK, and after a fall it did better. With two
events the sign-flip test cannot go below p = 0.5, and the plan requires 10
events for inference. With a daily excess-return volatility of 1.86%, two
events can only reveal an average move of about 3.7% or more, which would
be implausibly large.

### Family W, walk-forward: 0 of 18 pass

| Horizon | Out-of-sample forecasts |
|---|---|
| 1 session | 12 |
| 5 sessions | 4 |
| 20 sessions | 0 |

The gate needs 120. At the 1-session horizon the level-based forecasts did
**worse** than both naive baselines: out-of-sample R² of −0.10 for H100 and
−1.83 for B200.

### Gate condition G3, strategy: 0 of 18 pass

No strategy made more than 4 trades in its 12 out-of-sample windows, and
none has a Sharpe interval above zero. A round trip costs about 0.38%
against a daily excess-return volatility of 1.86%, so a tradable signal
would have to forecast moves of 0.4% or more a day.

### Family S, secondary: 1 of 54 passes

The one pass is the neocloud basket against the B200 level change, next
session:

- correlation −0.42 (95% CI −0.59 to −0.02);
- Benjamini-Hochberg q = 0.067;
- permutation p = 0.031, which is the smallest value 32 sessions allow.

**Post-hoc sensitivity check** (descriptive, not a test, logged in
`docs/variants_log.md`): leaving out the two sessions with large B200 moves
(09-04 and 10-05) drops the correlation to −0.12. Doing the same for the
basket against the H100 level drops it from −0.41 to −0.06. Both patterns
come from two days, and one of them is probably a composition effect.

### Reverse direction (diagnostic)

The H100 level change is about as correlated with the basket's excess return
on the session **before** the signal was known (−0.38) as with the session
after (−0.41). That is not what a clean "index leads equities" pattern looks
like. A common shock, or noise, fits better. With 5 non-zero values it
means little either way.

## What the evidence supports

1. **The test runs end to end on real data.**
   - Signals are dated by when the live system would have had them.
   - Outcomes use prices one could have traded at.
   - The pre-registered tests, gate, shadow log and daily report run
     without manual steps.
   - Tests on synthetic data confirm three things: the walk-forward cannot
     use an outcome before it is known, a planted effect is found, and a
     null world passes nothing.
2. **Our index is too sticky for daily inference.** List prices rarely
   change. The H100 level moved on 5 of 32 sessions and availability almost
   never. Most sessions carry no new information, so the effective sample
   is a handful of days.
3. **Costs set a high bar.** At the assumed costs a daily long-short trade
   needs a forecast edge of about 0.4%. That is a fifth of a typical day's
   excess-return move.

## What it cannot yet support

- **Direction.** Any claim that the index leads equities, or lags them.
- **Size or sign.** Any statement about the size or sign of a relation.
  Today's point estimates for the level signals are negative, but each
  rests on two to five days.
- **Longer horizons.** The 5- and 20-session horizons have almost no
  independent data.
- **Trading.** The gate needs at least 120 out-of-sample forecasts (G4).
  - The earliest date a frequently changing signal such as `disp_h100`
    could pass is about March 2027: 20 training sessions plus 120 forecasts
    after 2026-08-20.
  - Sparse signals also need 30 non-zero values out of sample. At today's
    rate that is late 2027 or later.
- **Kill criteria.** If no primary test passes by about August 2027 (250
  sessions), claim 4 is declared "not supported". If none passes by about
  August 2028 (500 sessions), it is rejected (K1, K1b).

## What would make the test more informative

Each of these would be a new variant, declared before it is run. None was
run.

- **A more responsive signal.** For example, the median change of matched
  listings, which removes composition effects like the 10-05 B200 move.
- **A longer history.** A licensed history of the settlement index (Silicon
  Data, blocker B7) would allow the same question to be asked over years
  instead of weeks.

## Paper trading status

- **Mode.** Shadow. The gate selected no pair. One prediction has been
  logged (session 2026-10-06), no order has been sent, and the paper account
  has no positions.
- **Limits, from config.** USD 10,000 per symbol, USD 20,000 gross, a
  USD 1,000 daily loss limit and a USD 3,000 drawdown latch.
- **Kill switch.** Set it in config or with `COMPUTE_CURVE_KILL_SWITCH=1`;
  it closes all claim-4 positions and blocks orders.
- **Daily operation.** The workflow `.github/workflows/daily.yml` re-runs
  the evaluation and the shadow log every day after the US close, once the
  owner merges it to the default branch and adds the Alpaca secrets
  (`docs/status.md`).

## Changes made after seeing the first run

D34 added five computational guards against small-sample artifacts:

- degenerate block bootstraps;
- Newey-West with the lag close to n;
- a biased Sharpe interval;
- the family size used by Holm and Benjamini-Hochberg;
- event intervals.

Each can only make a test stricter. The only outcomes that changed were two
G3 "passes" that were artifacts; both now fail. No definition or threshold
changed.
